## Abstract

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) are trained by optimising a composite objective that combines data fitting with physics-based constraints, typically resulting in a highly imbalanced multi-task optimisation problem. Under these conditions, existing optimisation strategies are affected by conflicting task gradients, leading to slow convergence and unstable training, particularly for stiff and high-frequency partial differential equations. We analyse gradient conflicts throughout training of PINNs with standard optimiser and investigate Multi-Task Deep Learning (MTDL) optimisation methods. 
In our analysis across four benchmark problems we observed that PINN optimisation exhibits three distinct phases in which angle- and magnitude-based gradient conflicts alternate, with only one present at a time.
Building on these observations, we propose PAM-GS, a physics-aware gradient surgery method that adaptively mitigates task interference during training according to the observed conflict types. 
Experiments on four representative PDE benchmarks demonstrate that PAM-GS combines competitive solution accuracy with consistently strong task-balanced performance, outperforming existing methods on most problems.

## Addressing Gradient Conflicts in PINNs

We study two distinct types of gradient conflict in PINN training: angle-based and magnitude-based.

**Angle conflicts** happen when task gradients point in different directions and partially cancel. They slow convergence but don't usually block it.

**Magnitude conflicts** happen when one task's gradient dominates the others. This biases training toward a single objective and can stall the rest entirely — the more damaging failure mode.

Tracking both conflict types during vanilla PINN training (Adam optimiser), we found they follow the same Fitting → Diffusion → Total Diffusion phase structure previously reported for inter-batch gradients — but here it emerges *within* a batch, across loss terms.

- **Fitting:** angle conflict is negligible, magnitude similarity is low
- **Diffusion:** angle conflict rises, magnitude similarity rises too
- **Total Diffusion:** angle conflict falls again, magnitude similarity degrades

Most existing gradient surgery methods (including SAM-GS) assume both conflicts co-occur and correct for them together — which breaks down when magnitude imbalance appears without angular disagreement.

### Our method: PAM-GS

**Physics-Aware Momentum Gradient Surgery** detects which conflict is present at each step and applies a matched fix:

- Magnitude conflict → equalise task gradients
- Angle conflict → reweight using momentum on gradient-magnitude similarity
- No conflict → aggregate directly

Magnitude conflicts are prioritised, since they're the ones that can stall training outright — angle conflicts only cost speed.
